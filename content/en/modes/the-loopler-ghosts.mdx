Ghost cars are the most misunderstood mechanic in The Loopler. Players see a second car lapping the track, assume it is free money, and then wonder why their leaderboard score looks wrong. Understanding how **The Loopler ghosts** work is the difference between a clean twelve-round clear and an accidental infinity chase. Because **The Loopler ghosts** cross the finish line and count loops just like your own slot car, every ghost you spawn is a decision, not a bonus.

## What Are The Loopler Ghosts?

Ghosts in The Loopler are secondary cars that run the track alongside your slot car. You do not pick them from a menu; they appear through cards and effects you collect during a run, then keep moving on their own once they are on the track. Everything they touch can affect your score, your gold, and your gate payouts.

That autonomy is the point and the problem. The Loopler ghosts behave like a second racer who never asks permission. Community reports describe players spawning ghosts to farm gate value, only to watch a ghost complete a lap and quietly add a loop they did not want.

The developer, Mheep, has tuned ghost-related cards across the launch patches, which tells you the mechanic is meant to be a real strategic lever rather than background decoration. If you are new to the game, read the [official Steam store page for The Loopler](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4150860/The_Loopler/) first, then come back — ghosts only make sense once you understand the core loop of score, fuel, and durability.

## What Ghost Cars Actually Do When They Cross the Line

The single most important fact about **The Loopler ghosts** is that a ghost crossing the finish line counts as a loop. This was confirmed directly in a Steam community discussion where a player asked whether ghost laps count. The answer from an experienced player: yes, ghosts that cross the line add a loop, and they can also trigger gates along the way.

That means a ghost is never neutral. It is either pushing you toward a higher loop count or, if you are chasing a low-loop score, actively working against you.

Gold is the part players trust most. Community reports consistently say gold collected by a ghost still lands in your pocket, which makes ghosts a reliable economy tool in mid-run builds. Gate interactions are broader than gold, though: a ghost passing a gate can set that gate off, which is why gate order and ghost timing are so tightly linked. Fuel and boost interactions are less clearly documented, so treat them as something to verify in your own runs rather than assume.

| Ghost event | Effect on the run | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Ghost crosses the finish line | Adds a loop | Confirmed by community reports |
| Ghost passes a gate | Gate triggers | Confirmed by community reports |
| Ghost collects gold | You receive the gold | Player experience |
| Ghost passes a boost gate | Boost interaction reported, details vary | Player experience — test it |
| Ghost runs out of track | Stops contributing | Player experience |

## Ghost Cars by Run Goal: Low-Loop Clears vs High-Loop Builds

The Loopler has more than one way to win, and ghosts are good for exactly one of them. If your goal is the lowest possible loop count, a ghost that reaches the finish line is a liability. If your goal is stacking loops for score, ghosts are one of the better tools in the game.

Community discussion frames it plainly: for low-loop scores you do not want ghosts passing the line, and for high-loop scores they are quite good at generating extra loops. A widely watched community video literally titled "I Used a GHOST CAR to BREAK the Loopler" shows the high end of that spectrum.

The practical takeaway is that you should decide your run goal before you place any ghost source, not after.

| Run goal | Do ghosts help? | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Lowest loop count clear | No — they hurt | Keep ghosts short of the finish line |
| Highest loop count | Yes — strongly | Let ghosts run full laps |
| Score-density build | Sometimes | Only if the ghost triggers high-value gates |
| Infinity push | Mixed | Weigh extra loops against score goal growth |
| Daily or weekly challenge | Depends on the objective | Read the challenge text first |

If you are chasing a settled leaderboard run, remember that community documentation notes leaderboard runs only count when you settle on round 12. Ghost loops that push you past that point can cost you the submission entirely.

## Ghost Cards, Gates, and Patch Changes

Ghosts do not exist in a vacuum — they are attached to cards and effects, and those have changed since launch. Patch V1.0.2, released September 19, 2026, one week after launch, included a card change stating that Effective Ghost now only speeds up ghosts spawned after it grows. That is a meaningful nerf for players who expected retroactive scaling.

The previous patch also fixed a ghost-related bug: Drift By Ghost was capping its speed bonus before it reached the ghosts. The V1.0.2 notes also fixed a display bug where the Ghost card showed decimals on the card itself while its tooltip rounded.

These are small lines in long patch notes, but they matter. If you built a ghost strategy from a guide written during launch week, some of its assumptions may no longer hold.

## Practical Tips and Common Mistakes With The Loopler Ghosts

Most ghost mistakes come from treating ghosts as passive income. They are active score events. The tips below are drawn from community reports and from how the mechanic has been tuned across patches.

**Tip 1: Decide your goal before you place a ghost source.** A ghost that is perfect for a high-loop score run is actively harmful in a low-loop clear.

**Tip 2: Watch gate order.** Gates and ghosts interact, so a ghost that fires a gate at the wrong point in the loop can waste a stored effect you were saving.

**Tip 3: Do not assume retroactive scaling.** Since V1.0.2, Effective Ghost only benefits ghosts spawned after it grows. Place your growth effects early if you want them to matter.

**Tip 4: Treat ghost gold as economy, not score.** Gold helps you buy pit stop upgrades, which compounds over a long run.

**Tip 5: Re-check old guides.** Two patches landed inside the first ten days, so launch-week ghost advice can be stale.

| Common mistake | What goes wrong | Better approach |
|---|---|---|
| Spawning ghosts in a low-loop run | Extra loops ruin the score | Delay or avoid ghost sources |
| Assuming ghosts refuel you | Fuel plan collapses | Keep a dedicated fuel source |
| Placing growth cards late | New ghosts do not benefit | Front-load Effective Ghost |
| Ignoring gate order | Gate value is wasted | Plan gate sequence around ghost laps |
| Trusting launch-week guides | Advice predates V1.0.2 | Verify against current patch notes |

## The Loopler Ghosts FAQ

**Do The Loopler ghosts count toward my leaderboard score?**
Yes, indirectly. A ghost that crosses the finish line adds a loop, and loops feed your score. On a low-loop leaderboard, that is a penalty. Community documentation also notes that leaderboard runs only count if you settle on round 12, so ghost loops that push you past a clean finish can cost you the submission.

**Can The Loopler ghosts be disabled once they are on the track?**
There is no simple off switch described in the available material. The practical approach is to control when you spawn them: if you do not want ghost laps, avoid the cards and effects that create ghosts during that run.

**What is the best use for ghost cars?**
Extra loops and extra gate triggers. Community reports confirm ghosts trigger gates and that ghost-collected gold reaches you, which makes them strong in high-loop score builds and in economy-focused runs where you want more pit stop spending power.

**Are ghosts good in infinity runs?**
It depends on your score density. Infinity reworked how the score goal grows, so raw loop count is not automatically valuable. If your ghosts fire high-value gates every lap, they can carry you. If they just add empty loops, they make the run harder for no return.

**Why did my ghost stop scaling?**
Likely Effective Ghost. Since V1.0.2, it only speeds up ghosts spawned after it grows, so ghosts created earlier will not benefit. Place growth effects before you spawn your ghosts, not after.

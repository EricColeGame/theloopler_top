## What The Loopler Is and Why Its Boards Matter

The Loopler launched on Steam on September 11, 2026, developed and published by Mheep. It's a roguelite deckbuilder with an unusual premise: instead of a hero or a spaceship, you're steering a slot car around mesmerizing loops. You pick cards, place gates on the track, and collect charms, then watch the numbers snowball until the screen is practically tearing apart.

That design is why leaderboards matter here. There's no final boss and no story ending — the whole point is "how far can you go in the pursuit of infinity." Once you can consistently finish a run, the only meaningful goal left is beating other people's numbers. Reviews back up the appeal: Steam shows an all-time rating of Very Positive, with 89% of 670 user reviews positive, and a recent 87% positive across 391 reviews.

The practical takeaway: a leaderboard score is never just about playing well. It's a build decision made across four systems at once.

## Every Board in The Loopler, Explained

Community reports and the game's own feature list point to several distinct ranking layers. They don't compete with each other — they measure different things, and confusing them is the fastest way to waste a good run.

| Board Type | What It Tracks | Reset Behavior | Best For |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Steam Leaderboards | Your best scores and run stats tied to your Steam account | Persistent | Long-term personal progression |
| Steam Stats | Aggregate numbers across your play history | Persistent | Tracking your own improvement curve |
| Daily challenges | A fixed seed and ruleset available for one day | Every 24 hours | Equal-footing competition |
| Weekly challenges | A longer-form challenge with its own optional board | Every 7 days | Bigger builds and deeper synergy |
| Speedrun.com | Fastest completions, split by category and settings | Persistent | Route optimization and record hunting |

Two things stand out. First, the daily and weekly challenges come with "a variety of optional leaderboards," meaning you can opt into the competitive layer or just enjoy the puzzle. Second, the Steam boards are account-bound, so they double as a personal history of every build you've ever pushed to its limit.

### Why the Optional Boards Are the Real Skill Test

A standard run lets you pick your car, your track, and your difficulty. A daily challenge usually locks at least some of that down. That removes the "I got a lucky seed" excuse and forces you to solve the same puzzle everyone else is solving. If you want to know how good you actually are at The Loopler, the rotating boards are the honest answer.

### How to Submit a Run

1. Record your full run from start to finish — no cuts.
2. Note your category, difficulty, car, and unlock setting before submitting.
3. Upload the video and submit it through the game's Speedrun.com page.
4. Wait for a moderator to verify. Community reports list several active moderators keeping the board clean.
5. If a run gets rejected, check the category rules first — most rejections come from mismatched settings, not from the time itself.

## Daily and Weekly Challenges: The Rotating Board

The daily and weekly challenges are where The Loopler leaderboard stays fresh after you've memorized every track layout. Each one gives you a fixed scenario, and each has optional rankings attached.

| Challenge Type | Cadence | Typical Approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily | Every 24 hours | One or two focused attempts, low risk, high repetition |
| Weekly | Every 7 days | Longer build planning, more synergy experimentation |

Because these reset, they reward consistency over raw talent. A player who spends fifteen minutes on every daily challenge for a month will out-rank a player who grinds one marathon session and burns out. Treat them as a warm-up routine: they keep your drafting instincts sharp and teach you card interactions you'd never discover on your own.

## Practical Tips to Climb The Loopler Leaderboard

These are the habits that show up again and again in strong runs. None of them require mechanical skill — The Loopler is a strategy game, so the advantage comes from planning.

| Tip | Why It Works | Time Investment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lock your car choice early | Switching cars mid-progression resets your mental model of scoring | Low |
| Plan gate order, not just gate choice | Placement slots and combo order decide how much each gate pays out | Medium |
| Chase one multiplier type | Stacking one effect compounds faster than spreading across three | Low |
| Hunt charms that change rules | Rule-changing charms outperform flat stat charms over long runs | Medium |
| Practice a single track | Route familiarity beats generalist knowledge on time-based boards | Medium |
| Watch top VODs | You'll see build paths you'd never draft yourself | Low |
| Cap your attempts | Endless retries degrade decision quality; short sessions stay sharp | Low |

### The Biggest Mistake New Players Make

They chase big scores when the board rewards speed, or chase speed when the board rewards score. Read the board first. If the category is time-based, your build should prioritize early acceleration and a fast route to the finish. If the board is score-based, you want slow compounding multipliers that only pay off deep into a run. Those two builds look almost nothing alike.

### Difficulty Is a Multiplier, Not a Wall

With five difficulty modes in the game, it's tempting to stay on the easiest one where you can reliably go infinite. Don't. Higher difficulties usually gate access to the boards that matter most, and the players at the top of those boards aren't superhuman — they've simply learned which builds survive the tighter constraints. Move up one difficulty at a time and rebuild your synergy from scratch rather than trying to force an old build to work.

## FAQ

### Is there a leaderboard in The Loopler?

Yes. The game supports Steam Leaderboards as a core feature, plus daily and weekly challenges that come with a variety of optional leaderboards. A separate community-run speedrunning board also exists on Speedrun.com for players who want to compete on completion times.

### What's the fastest recorded Infinity run on The Loopler leaderboard?

Community reports list a 5:17 Infinity run on Bronze Loop with the Yellow car and any unlocks as the fastest submission at the time of writing. Because the board is small and actively moderated, that time could change quickly.

### Do I need to unlock everything before competing?

No — and in some cases you shouldn't. Community submissions are split between "Any unlocks" and "All unlocks" for a reason. Several of the fastest listed times were set with any unlocks allowed, suggesting a smaller card pool can be more consistent than a fully unlocked one.

### How do I improve my rank without grinding for hours?

Focus on the rotating daily and weekly boards. They reset constantly, so a short, well-planned attempt every day beats one long session per week. Combine that with watching a top run or two to learn build paths, and you'll climb The Loopler leaderboard faster than pure repetition would ever get you.

The bottom line: the boards in The Loopler aren't a side activity — they're the reason the loop keeps pulling you back. Pick one board, learn its rules, and start optimizing. Infinity isn't going anywhere.

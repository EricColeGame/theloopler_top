## What Is The Loopler?

The Loopler is a single-player roguelite deckbuilder about a tiny slot car driving endless, hypnotic loops. Each run you draft cards, place gates on the track, and collect charms until the numbers on screen stop making any kind of sense. Developer Mheep pitches it as settling into the loop while the numbers climb and the dopamine flows — and that description is remarkably accurate.

The store listing tags it as Casual, Indie, Racing, Simulation, and Strategy. That genre soup sounds odd until you play it: the racing is automatic, the strategy lives in your build, and the "simulation" is really a giant spreadsheet of multipliers pretending to be a toy car set. It sits comfortably next to games like Balatro and Slay the Spire in the "one more run" category, but the aesthetic — bright tracks, skid marks, ghost cars — is entirely its own thing.

Runs are short, feedback is immediate, and the card triggers fire in rapid succession. If you like watching a build snowball into absurdity, this is built for you.

## How the Core Loop Actually Works

Understanding the three systems makes your first runs dramatically better. Here is how they interact.

| System | What It Does | How to Use It |
|---|---|---|
| Cards | Modify score, speed, fuel, and trigger effects | Draft for synergy, not raw numbers |
| Gates | Placed on track slots; fire when your car passes | Order matters — chain multipliers together |
| Charms | Passive modifiers that reshape a run | Stack them around one clear win condition |

Cards are where most of your decisions happen. Some add passive multipliers, some spawn ghost cars, some roll dice for jackpots, and some simply trigger other cards. The trick is that raw stat cards feel strong early and fall off late, while trigger-based cards look weak until they suddenly are not.

Gates are the placement puzzle. Each track gives you a fixed number of slots, and the order your car hits them determines the combo. A gate that multiplies score means nothing if it fires before the gate that generates score in the first place.

Charms are the long game. They are passive, they stack, and they are usually what separates a good run from a run where your car freezes because the numbers got too big. That is not a hypothetical — community reports describe cars stalling out on the track for 30 seconds at extreme speeds before the engine catches up.

## Tips for Your First Loops

A few things veteran players have learned the hard way:

- **Pick a direction early.** Big score or insane speed? Both work, but builds that chase both tend to stall out around the mid-game.
- **Read the fuel math.** Community reports flag the "+1 fuel cell per loop" ability as confusing — it has diminishing returns rather than a flat refill, which is a deliberate design choice to prevent infinite runs. Do not build around it as an unlimited engine.
- **Expect high-speed detection quirks.** Some players report gates occasionally not registering when the car is moving extremely fast. It is rare and generally cosmetic, but worth knowing before you blame your build.
- **Use the daily and weekly challenges.** They are a low-pressure way to test cards you would never draft in a normal run, and the optional leaderboards give you a benchmark.
- **Max out cards and gates before chasing infinity.** The endgame difficulty modes are tuned around fully upgraded builds.

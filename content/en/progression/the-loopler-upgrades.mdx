## What Counts as an Upgrade in The Loopler

If your car keeps stalling out around the midpoint of a run, the problem is rarely the individual card you picked — it is how you sequenced The Loopler upgrades around it. Upgrades in this slot-car roguelite deckbuilder live across four separate systems, and each one multiplies the value of the others. Getting The Loopler upgrades right is the difference between a run that sputters to a stop and one where your score multiplier tears across the screen.

Cards are drafted into your deck. Gates are physically placed into slots on the track. Charms are passive effects you collect and stack. Garage parts modify the car itself. Each layer talks to the others: a card that triggers on drift needs a gate to keep drift ticking at extreme speeds, and a score charm is only as good as the gate order that lets your car pass through the payoff slot more often.

| Upgrade layer | Where you get it | What it changes | Best time to invest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cards | Card packs and route rewards | Score multipliers, fuel, speed, card triggers | Every round — this is your build's core |
| Gates | Pit stop placement into track slots | What happens as the car crosses each slot | Early to mid run, once you learn the layout |
| Charms | Shops and reward choices | Run-wide passive effects | When a charm matches the engine you already have |
| Garage parts | The garage between rounds | Car-level modifiers and stats | When one specific stat is your bottleneck |

For the official rundown of what ships in the box — nine cars, nine tracks, five difficulty modes, 50 cards, 30 gates, and 70 charms — the [The Loopler page on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4150860/The_Loopler/) lays it out plainly. The game launched on September 11, 2026 at $9.99 and holds a Very Positive rating, which means there is a healthy community comparing builds.

## How Progression Unlocks Feed Your Upgrades

The Loopler upgrades you *can* take are gated behind progression, and the developer has been actively tuning that curve. Patch notes for the game's early updates describe adjusted XP gains so that completing rounds matters more than simply starting them, plus a rebalanced unlock cadence that pushes new mechanics at players sooner. A separate fix restored cards, charms, and gates unlocked from level 6 onward to the loadout automatically — a bug that had quietly starved mid-game players of tools they had already earned.

Two changes are especially relevant to planning:

- **Difficulties now unlock globally** rather than per track, so progress on one circuit carries across the whole roster.
- **Track cards open on your highest unlocked difficulty** instead of resetting to the easiest tier.
- **The Infinity choice unlocks after you beat any run**, not only the specific track you are on.
- **A one-time loadout tutorial** now appears when you first enter track select after unlocking the loadout system.

| Progression system | What it unlocks | Why it matters for your build |
|---|---|---|
| XP and levels | New cards, gates, and charms enter your loadout pool | Widens the number of viable upgrade paths |
| Global difficulty unlocks | Higher tiers with harsher penalties | Changes which upgrades stay efficient |
| Infinity access | Endless scaling after a settled run | Tests whether your engine truly compounds |
| Garage stats panel | Loop Score, Overdrive Timer, Overdrive Pulse, Lucky Chance | Lets you diagnose why a build underperforms |
| Achievements and challenges | Long-term goals and daily/weekly runs | Forces you to try upgrade combos you would skip |

## Upgrade Priority Tiers: What to Max First

Most players lose runs because they spread attention evenly. In practice, a small number of upgrade categories do the heavy lifting. Treat the list below as a default order and deviate only when a reward is unusually strong.

| Tier | What to prioritize | Why it comes first |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | A reliable fuel source — a fuel or brake gate | A run ends when the tank empties, not when score is low |
| Tier 2 | Score multiplier cards and the gates that feed them | Multipliers compound; flat score does not |
| Tier 3 | Gate placement order and slot synergy | The same gates in a different order produce very different payouts |
| Tier 4 | Charms that reinforce an existing engine | Strong alone, but only dominant when stacked correctly |
| Tier 5 | Cosmetic and comfort upgrades | Nice to have, irrelevant to reaching infinity |

One practical note from player experience: the first pit stop of a run is designed to offer a fuel or brake gate if you do not yet have a fuel source. That is a safety net, not a strategy. Taking fuel early and then building your multiplier engine around it is far more reliable than gambling on a late refuel.

## How Each Upgrade Layer Actually Compounds

### Cards

Card rarity matters more than most new players realize. Rarity names extend past Legendary, and charge counts on ON START and ON EMPTIED cards are displayed as information rather than as part of the effect. Selling certain cards — Punch Card and Write Off among them — now refunds the score multiplier they granted, so a "free" sell is never actually free.

### Gates

Gates are where The Loopler upgrades get genuinely strange. A Hungry Gate placed on top of an existing gate now eats it and absorbs its levels, which is how players push a single slot far beyond normal limits. Replacing a Multiply Gate removes the multiplier it granted, so plan your slots before you commit. Score Gates award half the base loop score before increasing that base, and Double Gates apply difficulty penalties only to their multiplier bonus above 1x — details that matter enormously on the hardest tiers.

### Charms and Lucky Chance

Lucky Chance is the quiet stat behind the best upgrade runs. Every 100% of Lucky Chance shifts the lowest rank a card pack can hand out, and at 100% or more it allows card upgrades to jump two ranks instead of one. If you are chasing rarity, this is the lever.

| Lucky Chance threshold | Effect on your upgrades |
|---|---|
| Below 100% | Packs can still hand out low-rank cards |
| At 100% | Lowest possible pack rank rises; upgrades can jump two ranks |
| Stacked high | Rarity floor keeps climbing, raising your average card quality |

### Garage Parts

Garage modifiers can be upgraded, and the stats panel now explains what each stat does rather than just naming it. Boost Cap shows the actual speed it allows in brackets after the percentage, and the Frozen Threshold tooltip clarifies that nothing can pool again until the cooldown after a freeze ends. If a build feels like it should be faster than it is, the garage panel is usually where the answer hides.

## Common Upgrade Mistakes Players Report

Community reports and negative reviews cluster around the same handful of problems, and most of them are fixable with better habits rather than better luck.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Spreading upgrades across every category | No single engine ever gets strong enough to scale | Pick one multiplier engine and feed it |
| Replacing gates casually | Multipliers granted by a replaced Multiply Gate are removed | Lock in your gate layout before upgrading |
| Ignoring fuel until the tank empties | The run ends regardless of your score | Take the Tier 1 fuel option early |
| Skipping garage stat tooltips | You cannot fix a bottleneck you cannot identify | Hover every stat at least once per run |
| Judging upgrades by feel alone | Effects are subtle and stack multiplicatively | Watch the score popups and multiplier pills |

One recurring critique in Steam reviews is that it is hard to tell whether an upgrade actually did anything — a "click and forget" feeling. The counter is to watch two numbers: your loop score and your multiplier pills. If neither moves after a purchase, the upgrade was probably misplaced rather than weak.

## FAQ

**Do The Loopler upgrades carry over between runs?**
No. Cards, gates, and charms are run-scoped and reset when a run ends. What persists is meta progression: XP, unlocks, difficulty access, and the loadout pool those unlocks feed. Treat each run as a fresh test of a build idea.

**How many upgrade layers should I manage at once?**
Four — cards, gates, charms, and garage parts. Trying to optimize all four from round one is a trap. Secure fuel, then build one multiplier engine, then let charms and garage parts reinforce it.

**What is the fastest way to unlock more The Loopler upgrades?**
Complete rounds rather than restarting early. XP gains were rebalanced so finished rounds count for more, and difficulty unlocks are now global, so pushing one track hard opens content everywhere.

**How do I know if a run is actually going well?**
Check whether your score per loop is growing faster than the round goal. If your multiplier pills are not rising, your build is flat, and no amount of gate tinkering will save it. Settle the run at round 12 if you want it to count on the standard leaderboards — scores are only submitted if the run is settled.

A slot car circles a track, gates flash as it passes, and your score climbs into the millions. That's the hook of The Loopler, a roguelite deckbuilder where you draft cards, place gates, and collect charms instead of steering. If you've bounced off after a few runs wondering what your upgrades actually did, you're not alone — that's the single most common complaint in community reports. This The Loopler tutorial exists to fix exactly that. Below you'll find a step-by-step The Loopler tutorial for building your first winning engine, plus tables that explain every gauge, trigger, and pit-stop decision you'll face.

## The Loopler Tutorial Basics: What You Actually Control

The car drives itself. It always has. Your real job is engineering: choosing which effects to buy, where to place them, and which route to take next. Understanding that split is the foundation of any good run.

| System | What it does | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cards | Drafted effects with printed triggers, such as on loop, on start, on emptied, or on adjacent cards | Your main source of score and multipliers |
| Gates | Effects placed into track slots that the car physically crosses | Placement order changes how combos resolve |
| Charms | Passive collectibles that add bonuses | Often carry run-defining payoffs |
| Garage parts | Between-run equipment that modifies stats like fuel tank, fuel efficiency, durability, Boost cap, and top speed | Permanent progress that softens later runs |
| Car effects | Every car has a unique effect | A starting bias, not an order to force matching items |
| Loadout | Controls which unlocked cards, gates, and charms are eligible for offers | Enabled items can appear; they are never guaranteed |

A round ends when you fill the score goal. Then you take a reward, pick a route on the map, and visit a pit stop or event before the next round begins. The car is on rails; the decisions are all yours.

## Build Your First Working Engine in Four Steps

The most reliable advice from experienced players is boring but effective: survive first, score second, multiply last. If you only remember one rule from this The Loopler tutorial, make it this — slow the simulation down and watch one complete loop before you buy anything.

| Priority | Goal | Example action | Warning sign |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Solve fuel survival | Take dependable refueling or fuel efficiency help | Tank empties and durability starts falling |
| 2 | Pick one scoring engine | Commit to loop scoring, a gate chain, or one repeated payoff | You own half of three different engines |
| 3 | Buy dependable triggers | A gate the car always crosses beats a charm waiting on a combo | Your effects rarely fire during a normal lap |
| 4 | Add multipliers | Increase frequency or payout of what already works | Big numbers on paper, flat score on the track |

**Step 1 — Watch a full lap.** Note which effects fire, whether fuel ends higher or lower, and how far the score goal moved. That single lap tells you whether you built an engine or a very fast problem.

**Step 2 — Choose one engine.** Loop-trigger scoring, a gate chain, or a setup that repeatedly feeds one payoff. Mixed builds feel flexible and usually stall around the mid-run.

**Step 3 — Spend on triggers, not dreams.** Gold spent on an effect that already fires every lap is never wasted.

**Step 4 — Multiply afterward.** Multipliers amplify a working machine. They cannot rescue a broken one.

## Cards, Gates, and Charms: Reading Triggers Like a Pro

Card text is where new players get lost, so read the trigger condition before the number. A card that fires on every loop and a card that fires once per repair are not comparable, even if their percentages look similar.

| Trigger type | When it fires | Best used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On loop | Every completed circuit | Steady score growth |
| On start | At the beginning of a round or repair cycle | Front-loaded bursts |
| On emptied | When a charge or resource runs out | Recycling depleted effects |
| On adjacent cards | Based on neighboring card slots | Tight, deliberate layouts |
| On repair | Each time the car is repaired | Long, attrition-heavy runs |
| Passive charm | Constantly while owned | Flexible, low-maintenance bonuses |

Gates deserve their own table, because placement changes everything.

| Gate | What it does | Practical tip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Score Gate | Awards half the base loop score, then raises that base | Excellent early pick for compounding growth |
| Multiply Gate | Multiplies score; the multiplier is removed if the gate is replaced | Place where the car crosses on every lap |
| Double Gate | Boosts the multiplier above 1x | Difficulty penalties apply only to that bonus portion |
| Hungry Gate | Eats a gate placed on top of it and absorbs its levels | Community reports show this can snowball fast |
| Drift Gate | Keeps drift ticking at extreme speeds | Effectively mandatory for drift builds |
| Mirror Gate | Replays other gate effects | Placement-dependent; test before committing |
| Chain Gate | Accumulates score | Remember the total resets each round |

A complete The Loopler tutorial could spend hours on gate ordering alone. For now, remember one principle: gates are board position, and position decides whether a combo pays out or fizzles.

## Reading the Gauges: Fuel, Durability, Boost, and Overdrive

Numbers go up, but the wrong gauge can still end your run. Here's what each meter is telling you.

| Gauge | What it does | Failure symptom | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fuel | Keeps the car running | Tank empties mid-round | Refuel cards, efficiency parts, fuel gates |
| Durability | Absorbs damage when fuel runs dry | Health drains during a lap | Repair at pit stops; protect fuel first |
| Boost / Boost cap | Raises speed | Excess boost decays during Overdrive | Raise the cap in the garage |
| Drift meter | Rewards cornering | Drift stops triggering at very high speeds | Add a Drift Gate |
| Overdrive | Repeating speed pulses on a timer | Pulses arrive too slowly to matter | Compare Pulse and Timer before investing |
| Lucky Chance | Improves card rarity and upgrades | Pack quality stays flat | Stack it past 100% for two-rank upgrades |
| Freeze threshold | Freezes the car briefly | Nothing pools until the cooldown ends | Plan around the cooldown, not through it |

Two details trip up almost everyone. First, drift only activates in corners — straights do nothing for it. Second, boost above your cap decays during Overdrive, while boost below the cap is retained, so raising the cap is often worth more than chasing raw gain.

## Pit Stops, Routes, and Difficulty Tiers

Pit stops are where runs are won. The first pit stop of a run always offers a fuel or brake gate if you don't yet have a fuel source, which is a quiet way of telling you what matters most.

| Difficulty | Rough feel | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bronze | Forgiving, great for learning | Penalties are mild |
| Emerald | Builds start to matter | Cards and gates lose effectiveness |
| Diamond | Punishing, narrow | Weak builds fail early |

A few structural rules worth internalizing:

- Difficulties unlock globally, not per track, and track cards now open on your highest unlocked difficulty.
- Route choices appear at the start of a run, then again at rounds 7 and 13.
- Leaderboard runs only count if you settle on round 12.
- The Infinity option unlocks once you've beaten any run, and scores are only submitted to normal leaderboards if the run is settled.
- Reroll costs climb quickly, so reroll only when you know exactly which role you're hunting.

You can follow patch history and community announcements on [The Loopler's official Steam community hub](https://steamcommunity.com/app/4150860), which is also where bug reports and balance discussions land first.

## Advanced Tips and Community Wisdom

Long-time players have surfaced several insights worth borrowing. Treat these as player experience rather than gospel, since balance changes with each patch.

- **Feeding a Hungry Gate is a real strategy.** Community reports describe stacking gates into it to inflate its levels far beyond normal limits.
- **Clarity is the biggest friction point.** Multiple players have asked for an in-game glossary because tooltips assume you already know the vocabulary.
- **Impact is hard to feel.** A recurring review theme is that upgrades are difficult to evaluate, unlike games where a build change is immediately visible. Watching one lap before and after a purchase solves most of this.
- **High difficulties narrow your options.** Several players report that Diamond runs heavily reward specific builds, so treat early shop rolls as critical on the hardest tiers.
- **Use the speed tools.** The 3x speed button and hold-R restart exist for a reason; grinding early rounds at normal speed burns patience fast.
- **Check the tutorial pages.** Recent patches added a one-time loadout tutorial and an Overdrive page explaining speed pulses, which directly address the confusion many new players feel.

## FAQ About The Loopler

**Do I need to steer the car at all?**
No. The car drives the circuit automatically, and your influence comes from cards, gates, charms, garage parts, and route choices. If you're waiting for a control input that never arrives, that's the game working as intended.

**What should I buy at my first pit stop?**
Fuel or efficiency, almost every time. A huge multiplier means nothing if the tank empties and durability starts draining. After fuel is stable, buy a trigger that already fires every lap.

**Does this The Loopler tutorial cover Infinity mode?**
It covers the foundation you need for it. Infinity unlocks after you beat any run, and it pushes score goals higher and harder over time. Get a stable engine first, then chase the infinite leaderboard.

**Why does my drift stop working at high speeds?**
Drift only triggers in corners, and it stops firing at extreme speeds unless you have a Drift Gate. Community guides flag this constantly, so grab one before your build depends on drift ticks.

Build small, watch one lap, protect the tank, and let the multipliers arrive once the machine actually runs. That's the whole lesson — and it's the one that turns a confusing first hour into a genuinely addictive loop.

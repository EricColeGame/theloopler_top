Reaching infinity in The Loopler is rarely about one lucky draft. A reliable The Loopler strategy is built on compounding multipliers, disciplined gate ordering, and charms that keep scaling long after the excitement of a fresh card fades. If your runs keep stalling in the teens or twenties on higher difficulty tiers, the problem is usually structural rather than random: your build stops growing while the score requirement keeps climbing. This guide pulls together player experience from the [official The Loopler page on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4150860/The_Loopler/) and community discussions to show which cars, gates, and charms actually carry a run — and which ones quietly waste your slots. Every solid The Loopler strategy starts with understanding that the game is a machine, not a shopping list.

## The Three Pillars: Cards, Gates, and Charms

The Loopler is a roguelite deckbuilder about a slot car driving mesmerizing loops. You draft cards, place gates in track slots, and collect charms, and each system feeds the other two. Treating them as separate shops is the fastest way to plateau.

| System | What it does | Best time to invest | What "good" looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cards | Drafted upgrades that change score, speed, multipliers, fuel, and trigger effects | Rounds 1–10, then selectively | At least one effect that scales every loop |
| Gates | Placed in track slots and triggered as the car passes | Mid game, once slots open up | Multiplier, double, and chain effects stacked in the right order |
| Charms | Passive modifiers that change how the run behaves | Whenever one appears | Compounding passives, not one-time flat bonuses |

Three rules cover most of it:

- **Scaling beats flat.** Anything that grows per loop outvalues a bigger fixed number.
- **Slots are currency.** Every gate slot spent on fuel or flat score is a slot not multiplying your run.
- **One trigger should feed another.** The game is designed around chain reactions, so build toward them deliberately.

## The Loopler Strategy for Picking a Car

There are nine cars, each with its own identity and effects. Community reports agree on one selection rule: choose a car whose specialty you cannot reproduce with cards and gates. If a car's bonus is something any build can assemble anyway, you are effectively playing without a specialty.

| Car (community reports) | Reported specialty | Replaceable by other setups? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frozen | Freeze-focused effects | No — freeze scaling is exclusive to it | Strong for long runs |
| Slot | Extra card and gate capacity | No — more slots compounds all game | Strong for long runs |
| Gold | Gold generation | Yes — many builds generate gold other ways | Situational |
| Drift | Drift-related effects | Yes — achievable through standard setups | Situational |
| Other cars | Varies by identity | Varies | Judge by uniqueness, not raw power |

That table reflects player experience rather than a tier list, and opinions shift as players experiment. The underlying principle holds: unique effects win, because duplicates of a common effect are just wasted identity.

## Gate Placement: Order Is the Whole Game

Gates trigger as your car passes them, so the sequence matters. A multiplier that fires before a big scoring gate multiplies more than the same multiplier placed after it. Players who go deep consistently report that their gate layout, not their card quality, is what separates a good run from an endless one.

| Gate | Reported effect | Why it scales | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scaling score gate | Score increases with each loop | Grows without needing new cards | S |
| Multiplier gate | Boosts score multiplier | Multiplies everything downstream | S |
| Double gate | Doubles output | Doubles whatever it touches | S |
| Chain gate | Triggers a chain of effects | Turns small triggers into cascades | S |
| Freeze gate | Freezes the loop | Extends scoring windows | A |
| Hungry gate | Converts or consumes for value | A community favorite for early carries | A |
| Refuel gate | Restores fuel | Needed early, replaceable later | B |
| Flat score gate | Adds a fixed amount of score | Falls off quickly | C |

A practical ordering habit: put anything that amplifies first, anything that generates score second, and anything that sustains the run (fuel, safety) last. Then re-check the order after every gate you add, because a new gate can change which sequence is optimal.

## Scaling, Freeze, and Fuel: Winning the Late Game

Freeze is one of the most repeated recommendations in community threads, and it comes with a real tradeoff. A higher freeze threshold means a longer freeze when it triggers, but also a longer cooldown before you can freeze again. That makes threshold a playstyle decision, not a straight upgrade.

| Freeze threshold | Freeze duration | Cooldown | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | Short | Short | Frequent small pauses, early rounds |
| Medium | Balanced | Balanced | General-purpose play |
| High | Long | Long | Late-game burst windows in front of big score gates |

Fuel is the other late-game trap. Community consensus is that by the mid-to-late game you only need one refuel source, and several players prefer a fuel **card** over a fuel **gate** specifically because it frees a gate slot for a multiplier. Two fuel sources are usually one too many — that is a The Loopler strategy detail that quietly costs people runs.

## A Round-by-Round Roadmap

If you want a repeatable plan rather than a pile of tips, use this as a default and adjust for the car you picked.

| Phase | Typical rounds | Priorities | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | 1–10 | Grab scaling cards, cheap multipliers, one refuel source | Over-investing in flat score |
| Mid game | 11–20 | Open gate slots, place multiplier/double/chain gates, take compounding charms | Splitting fuel across multiple sources |
| Late game | 21+ | Maximize freeze uptime, stack charms, re-order gates for amplification | Drafting flat bonuses that do nothing |
| Push | Infinity attempts | Re-check every slot and trigger, remove dead weight | Assuming your build is finished |

Notice that the roadmap is mostly about subtraction. Most failed runs are not missing a miracle card; they are carrying three mediocre gates and two redundant fuel sources.

## Common Mistakes That Kill Good Runs

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Ignoring scaling gates | Fixed values cannot keep pace with rising score goals | Prioritize gates and cards that grow per loop |
| Doubling up on fuel | Wastes a gate slot that could multiply | Keep one refuel source, ideally a card |
| Placing amplifiers late in the order | Multipliers apply to less | Move amplifying gates earlier |
| Picking a replaceable car | You lose a free edge every run | Choose unique specialties like freeze or extra slots |
| Freezing on reflex | Long cooldowns can miss your biggest windows | Save freezes for high-value gates |

One more note on unlocks: community members report that later unlocks are powerful but not required, with at least one player reaching infinity well before unlocking everything. If you are struggling early, your build structure is a better suspect than your collection.

## FAQ

**Do I need late-game unlocks to make The Loopler strategy work?**
No. Players report reaching infinity with a modest unlock pool. Unlocks expand your options, but scaling gates, freeze timing, and a single refuel source carry runs on their own.

**What is the best car for going infinite?**
Community reports favor cars with exclusive effects — freeze and extra card/gate capacity come up most often. Cars whose specialty can be replicated by cards and gates are situational picks.

**How many refuel sources should I run in the late game?**
One. A fuel card is generally preferred over a fuel gate because it keeps your gate slots free for multipliers.

**Should I chase score or speed?**
Score gates win runs. Speed matters mainly as a way to trigger gates more often, so treat it as a multiplier on your layout rather than a goal in itself.

Get the fundamentals right — scaling first, one fuel source, amplifiers early in the order, and a car with a real specialty — and infinity stops feeling like luck.
